# Scoped content detection

Read the full saved draft or script in its delivery context. A pattern is a finding only when it weakens the requested meaning, evidence, voice or reading/listening experience. Record an exact quote, native text/script location and saved revision. Classify each candidate as confirmed, intentional, protected or unverified; only confirmed findings within existing edit authority qualify for repair.

| Candidate | Confirm before editing | Preserve or reject the finding when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interchangeable praise or abstract benefit | The claim could describe any product and hides an available concrete mechanism or consequence. | An approved slogan, quote or deliberately broad opening has a documented role. Never manufacture a number or feature to replace it. |
| Long setup, faux revelation or rhetorical contrast | The setup repeats the point, delays the useful fact or implies unsupported expertise. | A personal story creates context; a contrast explains a real distinction; dramatic pacing is intentional in the script. |
| Vague authority or inflated significance | A factual claim relies on unnamed evidence, or commentary replaces evidence already available. | The source is named and retained, uncertainty is meaningful, or the text quotes someone else's claim. Retrieve support or omit/flag the claim. |
| Synonym cycling or repeated sentence shapes | Switching nouns obscures the same actor/object, or repeated shapes flatten the passage. | Repetition supports memory, a motif, parallel comparison, humor or deliberate spoken cadence. |
| Artificial punch lines or recap endings | The ending restates the piece or adds an unsupported metaphor instead of closure. | A CTA, summary, educational recap, poem or brand line is requested and earns its place. |
| Decorative formatting or punctuation | Bold, headings, bullets, emoji, colon reveals or dash clusters reduce reading clarity. | Formatting helps navigation, comparison, accessibility or the brand voice; punctuation and fragments serve the writer's rhythm. |
| Timing or format mismatch | Saved captions, titles or narration cannot be read/heard in the allotted time or diverge from the actual visual sequence. | Sparse text, silence or repetition is intentional and matches the brief and playback. |

No word blacklist, authorship probability or uniform house voice. Grammar examples and quoted source material are not candidate copy to rewrite. English phrase matching misses other languages and can misread idioms; inspect the actual language and localization before drawing a conclusion.

For a repaired draft, re-read the complete saved revision. Check that protected words and citations survive, no unsupported claims were added, the core point remains intact and the writer's distinctive voice remains recognizable. In video, check the repaired copy in playback. For review-only requests, return located findings and stop without rewriting.
