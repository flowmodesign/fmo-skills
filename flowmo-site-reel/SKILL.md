# Direct a website reel

The site is the footage. The reel's job is to make its best moments land for someone scrolling a feed with the sound off, then send them to one action.

## Find the story before writing a word
- Watch the whole page first. Name its idea in one line (a page that counts from 0 to 1 is a "zero to one" story; a product tour is "problem, the product doing it, proof, act"). The script follows the page's own idea, never a generic accelerator/product template.
- List the moments that MOVE: transitions, pinned stages, text that lights up, scrubbed films, counters. A scroll-driven page only performs while you scroll it, so these are the shots. Static stretches (long text, lists, footers, blogs) are skipped with a cut.
- Open on the most striking moving moment, not on a menu bar or a logo. The first two seconds must already be moving.

## Camera (the scroll)
- One continuous downward scroll; cut forward to skip, never scroll back up.
- Slow down where the page animates: about one screen height per 2-3 s through scroll-driven motion, so it plays at its intended pace. Faster reads as a blur.
- Hold 0.8-1.5 s where a reveal completes (a card fully formed, a headline fully lit, a number counted up). Leave a moment before the voice moves on.
- Never end a scene mid-transition; end on the settled state.
- Do not let the end of the page (blog lists, footer, legal) into the reel.

## Voice-over
- About half the reel voiced, one short line per scene at most, about 2.5 words per second. Silence over a strong moment is a choice, not a gap.
- Each line adds what the picture cannot say (why it matters, the outcome); it never reads the page's own text aloud word for word when that text is already on screen.
- Every fact comes from the page or the brief. No invented numbers, comparisons with competitors, or claims about "most accelerators/companies".
- The last line is the call to action, said plainly.

## Editing: what makes it feel cut, not recorded
- Zoom on purpose. A push-in (1.25-1.6x) onto the subject of a reveal while it happens, held, then released, is what makes a scroll feel edited. Use 3-5 in a reel: on the hook moment, on the key fact, on the payoff. Ease in and out (about 0.6-0.9 s); never zoom while the page is scrolling fast; never zoom so far that the subject leaves the frame.
- Hold every distinct state. A sequence (a counter ticking, digits forming 0 then 2 then 1, cards dealt one by one) needs a hold of about 0.8-1.2 s on EACH state, not one hold at the end. Rushing a sequence is the most common way to waste the page's best moment.
- Speed changes: move quickly through connective scroll, decelerate into a reveal, settle, then leave. Never a constant crawl.
- Entrances ease out, exits ease in; overlays enter in 0.3-0.5 s and leave faster. Every element has an entry, a hold long enough to read, and an exit.

## Overlays (graphics on top of the page)
- Kinetic captions carry the message for muted viewers: 2-6 words, set big, words arriving one by one; one accented word at most. Place them where the page is quiet, and never on top of text the page is animating itself.
- Stamps mark progress or a key number in the site's own style (a "DAY 7" badge, "$0"): they echo something the page shows, at the moment it shows it.
- Chips name the chapter in 1-4 words; tags carry ONE fact visible on screen (a price, a duration). Use chips OR captions in a scene, not both fighting for the eye.
- Rings only on a still element worth the eye (a price, a key number, the CTA button), never on something moving fast.
- Overlays wear the site's own design system: its typeface, its accent colours, its corner radius, its shadow style (a hard offset "sticker" shadow if the site uses one). They should look like part of the site, not a generic template.
- Sized for a phone: a viewer must read every word in well under a second.

## Sound
- Music: describe the bed in the site's own world (an 8-bit site gets chiptune, a calm product gets soft keys): instrumental, its tempo, and its arc - a short intro that lifts as the hook lands, a steady body, a lift for the payoff, a clean ending.
- Accents: a whoosh under each cut or big transition, a soft blip or hit on each reveal or stamp, a pop on the call to action. Few and timed to the picture; never a sound on every second.

## Intro and end card
- Intro title: the hook in a few words. Never the URL or host name.
- End card: a short headline in the viewer's terms (the outcome or the CTA), one button label of 1-3 words ("Apply now"), and the site's real public domain if the page shows it (footer email, links); a staging or preview host is never shown. The internal goal of the brief is never on screen.

## Length
Fit the target length by cutting scenes and trimming lines, never by slowing the footage to fill time. A scene with a line must last at least as long as its line.
