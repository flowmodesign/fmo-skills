# Action and performance

Identify the subtype before choosing a generator: contact action, locomotion/sport, choreographed dance, dialogue or presenter. These are different tests, even when the primary task is action or dialogue. Specify participants, their reference identities, starting positions, screen direction, framing, duration and final state.

Contact action: write an ordered cause/contact/reaction/recovery sequence. Separate simultaneous actions into shots when the clip cannot make the interaction readable. Keep feet grounded, hands attached to their bodies, contact points visible and momentum credible. A fast cut or camera shake must not hide broken interaction. Inspect before, at and after contact, not only a flattering thumbnail.

Sport and dance: establish cadence, direction, weight transfer, repeated movement and relationship to the camera. Describe the full-body action before inserting detail shots. Review each limb through occlusions and check drift, sliding feet and impossible joint changes. Slow motion is a timing requirement, not just the adjective "cinematic".

Dialogue: define speaker count, language, exact words, acting intention, pauses, eyeline and turn taking. Distinguish silent performance, generated speech and an existing audio track requiring lipsync. Check that the endpoint supports that exact combination. If lipsync is a separate stage, reserve its cost and keep the voice source bound to the correct speaker. Review audible words and intermediate mouth movement, not merely whether an audio track exists.

Presenter: control direct eyeline, framing, gestures, wardrobe and safe areas for separate editable captions. Avoid baked graphics when titles must remain editable. Review pronunciation, facial continuity and unnatural pauses in the full delivered clip.

Keep subtype, identity requirements and audio mode in the model-selection brief. Do not infer these capabilities from a general video ranking. Repair or change the route when the real clip fails its subtype checks.
