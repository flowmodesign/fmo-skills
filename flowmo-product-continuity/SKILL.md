# Product and continuity

Build a compact identity sheet from the actual supplied assets: silhouette and dimensions, materials, distinguishing geometry, logo and exact label placement, reference viewpoints, environment, lighting and palette. Separate facts visible in the references from proposed art direction. Never substitute the onboarding example for the user's product.

Classify the job more narrowly: catalog preservation, packaging/readable label, commercial hero, mechanism demonstration, matched character or matched architecture. These have different acceptance gates and can need different compatible models.

Catalog/packaging: preserve proportions, edge details, label copy, print alignment and contact shadows. Review at delivery resolution. If a mark must be exact and editable, keep it as a native overlay or use an appropriate editing workflow; do not promise that generated lettering is exact.

Commercial hero: choose a lens, camera path, surface, highlight travel and physical scale. Distinguish a moving camera from a moving or rotating product. Define what the reveal should make legible. Reject melted edges, drifting labels and reflections that change the object's form.

Mechanism: describe the actual moving parts and ordered mechanical action. Do not invent functionality. Check joints, hinge positions, contact, occlusion and end state across intermediate frames.

Matched shots: preserve identity and spatial geometry while naming the permitted changes. Start/end-frame conditioning, identity references and style references are separate roles; bind each only to supported sockets. For day/night, retain architecture, lens and composition while changing motivated light. For character cuts, check wardrobe, face and scale independently of the grade.

Record the continuity anchor and permitted change in each shot brief before routing. Review the assembled cuts as well as individual assets: different models may pass independently and still fail together. Reuse accepted anchors and assets rather than regenerating them on every recovery.
